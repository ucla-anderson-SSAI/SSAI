# MGMT298D Case Document Style Guide

## Overview

This style guide ensures consistency across all case documents for MGMT298D: Science and Strategy of AI. The formatting mirrors the slide deck style guide to create a cohesive visual identity across course materials.

---

## Writing Style

### Tone and Voice

Write in plain, clear prose with a balanced, academic tone. Sentences should be well-structured, with subordinate clauses that connect ideas and build on one another logically. Weave concrete details and specific numbers into flowing prose rather than isolating them in short declarative statements. Let the facts carry the weight, but give the reader enough syntactic connective tissue to follow the argument without effort.

Avoid both marketing buzzwords and telegraphic, staccato sentences. A sequence of three or more short declarative sentences in a row reads like a slide deck, not a case study. When listing related facts, combine them into a single sentence with appropriate conjunctions or semicolons.

Good: "Pricing is typically viewed as a business decision: set a margin, adjust for competitors, and hope for the best. But at scale, this approach fails because the optimal price for any product depends on a web of interrelated factors that shift continuously. Inventory levels, competitor prices, seasonal demand, and customer browsing behavior all influence willingness to pay, and they interact in ways that change by the hour. No pricing team, however large, can evaluate millions of products continuously while accounting for these interdependencies."

Bad (buzzy): "H&M faces a massive challenge: predicting what millions of customers will want across a sprawling global empire of stores."

Bad (staccato): "The optimal price depends on many factors. Inventory levels change. Competitor prices move. Demand fluctuates. No team can track all of this."

### Key principles

- Use American English spelling (colors, behavior, standardized)
- Prefer short, declarative sentences. Vary sentence length for readability but default to concise.
- Avoid rhetorical questions in the narrative. Reserve questions for the Discussion Questions section.
- Do not use bullet points or numbered lists in the narrative. Write in paragraphs.
- Each case should motivate why ML/AI unlocks value for the firm in question. Frame the business problem first, then the AI response.
- Keep hard numbers grounded in sources. Every statistic in the narrative should have a corresponding entry in the Sources section.
- Do not give away answers to discussion questions in the narrative. Present the facts and let students draw conclusions. For example, if a question asks why Company A survived and Company B didn't, the narrative should describe both outcomes without explaining the reason.
- Use no more than 2 em dashes per document. Prefer commas, periods, or semicolons.

### Narrative length

Target approximately 600-750 words of narrative text. The case should be readable in 3-4 minutes. With questions and sources, the full document should fit on 2 pages.

## Content Structure

### Purpose

Each case maps to a specific week's lecture topic. The case should serve as motivation for the ML/AI concepts covered that week. The narrative should make students feel why the problem matters before revealing the technical response.

### Narrative arc

Structure the narrative in two sections:

1. **The Problem** — What business challenge does the company face? Why does prediction (or classification, or optimization, etc.) matter for this specific business model? What went wrong or what opportunity exists? Use a descriptive heading that captures the core tension (e.g., "The Fast Fashion Prediction Problem").

2. **The Company's Response** — What did the company build? What data do they use? What were the results? Keep this concise and strategic — students need to understand what inputs the system uses and what outputs it produces, not the technical architecture. Use a heading like "[Company]'s Response."

### Discussion Questions

- Exactly 4 questions, each one or two sentences long
- Use "Question 1", "Question 2", etc. as subheadings
- Keep questions focused on ML/AI concepts and business strategy — not organizational politics or change management
- Questions should scaffold from foundational to more challenging:
  - Q1: Why does prediction/ML matter for this specific business? (the "so what")
  - Q2: What should the model predict, and at what level of detail? (outputs)
  - Q3: What data should the company collect? (inputs/features)
  - Q4: What are the limits or risks of this approach? (challenges)
- The question order above is a guideline — adjust to fit the case. But each case should cover these four themes.

### Sources

- 4-5 sources maximum
- Every hard number in the narrative must have a corresponding source
- Remove redundant sources that cover the same fact
- Format: "Title." Publication, Date. url
- Font: Arial, 10pt, regular, gray (#666666)

## Page Layout

- **Page size**: US Letter (8.5" x 11")
- **Margins**: 1" all sides
- **Line spacing**: 1.15 for body text
- **Paragraph spacing**: 10pt after paragraphs
- **Target length**: 2 pages maximum

## Document Structure

### 1. Header Line
- Course identifier: "MGMT298D: Science and Strategy of AI | UCLA Anderson"
- Font: Arial, 10pt, italic, gray (#666666)
- Gold (#FFD100) underline rule beneath

### 2. Document Title
- Case name as main title
- Font: Whitney, 20pt, bold, dark blue (#005587)
- Spacing: 18pt before, 12pt after

### 3. Week Subtitle
- Format: "Week N: Topic Name"
- Font: Arial, 11pt, italic, gray (#666666)
- Spacing: 3pt before, 18pt after

### 4. Section Headings
- Use descriptive headings (e.g., "The Fast Fashion Prediction Problem", "H&M's Response")
- Font: Whitney, 14pt, bold, dark blue (#005587)
- Spacing: 15pt before, 9pt after

### 5. Question Headings
- Format: "Question 1", "Question 2", etc.
- Font: Arial, 12pt, bold, dark blue (#005587)
- Spacing: 12pt before, 6pt after

### 6. Body Paragraphs
- Font: Arial, 12pt, regular, body text (#333333)
- Line spacing: 1.15
- Spacing: 10pt after each paragraph

### 7. Sources Section
- Font: Arial, 10pt, regular, gray (#666666)
- Spacing: 6pt after each source
- Format: "Title." Publication, Date. url
