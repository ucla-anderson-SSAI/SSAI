# MGMT298D Case Document Style Guide

## Overview

This style guide ensures consistency across all case documents for MGMT298D: Science and Strategy of AI.

---

## Writing Style

### Tone and Voice

Write in plain, direct prose. The register is a smart colleague explaining something clearly. Sentences should be well-structured. Weave concrete details and specific numbers into flowing prose rather than isolating them in short declarative statements.

Avoid three failure modes:

1. **Buzzy/journalistic** — dramatic framing, breathless pacing, LinkedIn energy. No "became front-page news," "raised serious questions," "harder to ignore." State what happened and let the reader judge.

2. **Staccato** — three or more short declarative sentences in a row reads like a slide deck. Combine related facts into a single sentence with conjunctions or semicolons.

3. **Wordy/padded** — cut filler phrases like "a combination of," "at a level of detail that," "on their own," "well enough." If a sentence works without the phrase, remove it.

Good: "This worked when the company was smaller and fashion cycles moved more slowly, but as H&M expanded globally and trends began shifting faster, the manual process could not keep up."

Bad (buzzy): "But as H&M expanded globally and the pace of trends accelerated — driven in part by social media — the limits of this approach became harder to ignore."

Bad (staccato): "The optimal price depends on many factors. Inventory levels change. Competitor prices move. Demand fluctuates. No team can track all of this."

Bad (padded): "Their task was to build models that could forecast demand at a level of detail that human planners could not realistically achieve on their own."
Better: "Their goal was to forecast demand at a granularity that human planners could not match."

### Key principles

- American English spelling (colors, behavior, standardized).
- Prefer short, declarative sentences. Vary sentence length but default to concise.
- No rhetorical questions in the narrative. Reserve questions for the Discussion Questions section.
- No bullet points or numbered lists in the narrative. Write in paragraphs.
- Frame the business problem first, then the AI response.
- Every specific number must trace to a listed source. If a statistic cannot be verified, describe the result qualitatively.
- Do not give away answers to discussion questions in the narrative. Present facts and let students draw conclusions.
- No more than 2 em dashes per document. Prefer commas, periods, or semicolons.

### Narrative length

Target 600-750 words of narrative text, readable in 3-4 minutes. Full document (with questions and sources) should fit on 2 pages.

## Content Structure

### Narrative arc

Two sections:

1. **The Problem** — What business challenge does the company face? Why does prediction (or classification, or optimization) matter here? What went wrong or what opportunity exists? Use a descriptive heading (e.g., "The Fast Fashion Prediction Problem").

2. **The Company's Response** — What did the company build? What data do they use? What were the results? Keep it concise and strategic: inputs and outputs, not technical architecture.

### Discussion Questions

- Exactly 4 questions, each one or two sentences.
- Subheadings: "Question 1.", "Question 2.", etc.
- Focus on ML/AI concepts and business strategy, not organizational politics.
- Scaffold from foundational to challenging:
  - Q1: Why does prediction/ML matter for this business?
  - Q2: What should the model predict, and at what level of detail?
  - Q3: What data should the company collect?
  - Q4: What are the limits or risks (overfitting, model complexity)?
- Adjust order to fit the case, but cover all four themes.

### Sources

- 4-5 sources maximum.
- Every hard number must have a corresponding source.
- Format: "Title." Publication, Date. url
- Font: Arial, 10pt, regular, gray (#666666).

## Instructor Guide Formatting

Each case has a companion instructor guide with teaching notes.

### Note Boxes
- Rendered as shaded table cells with a left accent border.
- Background: light blue-gray (#F0F4F8). Left border: dark accent (#2C5F7C).
- Note header: Arial, 11pt, bold, dark accent (#2C5F7C).
- Bullet text: Arial, 11pt, regular, black. No italics.
- Maximum 3 bullets per question. Each bullet is a concise prompt or key point, not a mini-essay. If you need more than 3 points, consolidate.

### Sections
- **Purpose of This Case** — 1 note box with big-picture framing, 1 note box with timing guidance.
- **Discussion Questions & Teaching Notes** — each question followed by a note box with up to 3 key points.
- **Transition to Lecture** — suggested segue language and a concept map (question to lecture topic).

### Writing Style for Teaching Notes
- Same plain, direct tone as the case itself.
- No jargon unless the jargon is the point (e.g., "this is where you introduce the term 'overfitting'").
- Bullets should tell the instructor what to push on, not summarize the case.
- State the key insight in one sentence, then add context if needed.
